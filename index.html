How phishing works

The attack is usually simple. The psychology is the real weapon.

🛡
🎭

Impersonation

Attackers copy a bank, coworker, school portal, delivery service, or cloud app to look legitimate.

🕒

Pressure

They add urgency like "verify now," "payment failed," or "account locked" to reduce careful thinking.

🖱️

Action

The message tries to get a click, a download, a reply, or a login submission.

🔒

Outcome

Once a victim acts, the attacker can steal passwords, MFA codes, money, or internal access.

Examples

Compare realistic phishing lures with the clues they expose.

Phishing

Fake password reset

From: Microsoft Security <security-team@micr0soft-login.co>

"Your password expires today. Failure to act in 30 minutes will disable your mailbox. Confirm your account immediately."

  • 🔗Lookalike domain uses `0` instead of `o`
  • ⏱Artificial deadline creates panic
  • 🔒Pushes an immediate credential action
Legitimate

Routine service notice

From: IT Support <support@company.com>

"Scheduled maintenance starts Friday at 10:00 PM. No action is required. Use the internal portal if you need status updates."

  • 🏢Normal domain matches the organization
  • ✓No pressure to click or disclose secrets
  • ⓘProvides context instead of fear
Phishing

Package delivery scam

Text message: "Delivery failed. Pay redelivery fee now."

The site asks for card details, billing address, and a one-time passcode for a tiny fake fee.

  • 💳Unexpected payment demand
  • 📋Requests more data than needed
  • $Uses a small fee to justify card entry
Phishing

Fake ID suspension email

From: Citizen ID Office <alerts@national-id-review.info>

"Your national ID will be suspended within 24 hours due to verification failure. Confirm your ID number and upload a photo immediately."

  • 🔗Sender domain does not match an official government address
  • ⏱Threatens suspension to force quick action
  • 📋Requests sensitive identity data and document upload
Phishing

Fake LinkedIn connection request

From: LinkedIn Network <connect@linkedin-careers.net>

"A recruiter wants to connect with you. View the private message and sign in now to avoid losing the opportunity."

  • 🔗Uses a lookalike LinkedIn domain instead of the real platform
  • ⏱Pushes urgency around a job opportunity
  • 🔒Designed to drive a credential-stealing sign-in
Legitimate

Legitimate bank notification

From: First National Bank <alerts@firstnationalbank.com>

"A new statement is available in your secure banking inbox. Please sign in through the mobile app or your usual bookmarked banking site."

  • 🏢Sender domain matches the bank's normal domain
  • ✓Directs users to trusted channels instead of a surprise link
  • ⓘProvides a routine account update without asking for credentials by email

How a polished fake login page steals trust

🏢

Impersonate a trusted portal

The attacker copies familiar branding and labels the page as a security check.

⚠

Create urgency

"Restore access" language pushes the user to act before inspecting the URL carefully.

🔑

Capture credentials

The form harvests email and password directly, without needing malware or an exploit.

⟳

Reuse access elsewhere

Stolen credentials can be replayed against email, payroll, cloud storage, or VPN logins.
